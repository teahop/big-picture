Industry sectors by employment

    About the data

    The map is a terrain of US industries, built only from public data and laid out by projected growth and size. Values marked ≈ are estimates made for this map.

    How to read it

    Left to right is projected employment growth from 2025 to 2035: shrinking industries on the left, the fastest growing on the right. Front to back is size, with the smallest in front and the largest at the back; Jobs and Value added each have their own arrangement, so switching moves the hills. Both axes are compressed so the map is not crowded into a corner: size is on a log scale, and growth runs between the 5th and 95th percentile of each level, so the few industries beyond it sit at the edge (their details say so). Each detailed industry is pulled 30% of the way toward its sector, and each deep-zone category toward its industry, so a little grouping survives, and hills too close together are nudged apart. The axis ticks are therefore a guide, not an exact reading. Sectors are drawn as hills; Industries as a topographic map seen from above, with contour lines (every fifth is heavier), the axes in the margins, and a career shown as rainbow bands on its hills, from violet at the foot to red at the top. Deep zones are a sea chart: size is depth, so the largest categories are the deepest water, and a career is shown as schools of golden fish, more fish for a larger share. Drag to move the map, scroll to zoom; zooming out past the whole map returns to the hills. Every industry raises the ground; its height is its size. Jobs and Value added switch between employment and value added. Heights are compressed (size to the power 0.35) so that small industries still form visible hills, and each hill shows only its own industry: a crowd of small industries never adds up to a taller mountain. Research and academia are not drawn separately; they appear as industries, such as colleges and universities and scientific research and development services.

    Your career on the map

    Add your CV reads a CV (a PDF or pasted text) or lets you fill in your roles and degrees by hand. The text is sent to this site's reader, which asks a language model (OpenAI) to list the roles and degrees and suggest an industry for each; nothing is stored, by the site or in your browser. Your career is drawn straight away; Make adjustments in the panel lets you check each row's industry and years. Wildflowers then cover each hill by its share of your career: years in each role, with recent roles counting more (a role's weight halves every eight years since it ended), and each degree counting as two years. Clear CV removes it. The match is a guess you corrected, not an assessment.

    Similar industries

    Position no longer shows similarity: two neighbours on the map have similar growth and size, not necessarily similar work. The details of each industry still list its most similar industries, by a similar, distinctive mix of occupations.

    Sources

    • Industries, employment, growth: BLS National Employment Matrix, 2025 and projected 2035 (NAICS 2022).
    • Value added: BEA GDP by Industry, 2025. The sector figures are BEA's; below the sector level each BEA industry is split by payroll (BLS QCEW, 2025), so those figures are ≈ estimates. Owner-occupied housing is excluded. Federal value added is mostly government pay and has no uniformed military in the job counts, so its value per job reads high.
    • Similarity: occupation mixes from the Employment Matrix, occupation descriptions from O*NET 31.0, and text-embedding similarity.
    • Deep zones (Level 2): a few industries are split finer than any official table. Sizes come from QCEW 2025 employment, IPEDS 2023 staff and published headcounts, all ≈ estimates.

    Levels

    Level 0 shows 20 industry sectors. Zoom in, or use the Detail control, for 304 detailed industries (Level 1), and then the deep zones (Level 2). Finer nodes replace their parent.

    Limits

    This is a descriptive map of the present, not a forecast or advice. Classification boundaries, estimates and similarity scores all carry error; the sources and rules are in the project's repository (data/SOURCES.md, SPEC.md).
