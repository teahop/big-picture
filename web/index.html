Industry sectors by employment

    About the data

    The map is a terrain of US industries, built only from public data, so that nearby industries are similar. Values marked ≈ are estimates made for this map.

    How to read it

    Every industry raises the ground; its height is its size. Jobs and Value added switch between employment and value added. Heights are compressed (size to the power 0.35) so that small industries still form visible hills, and each hill shows only its own industry: a crowd of small industries never adds up to a taller mountain. Research and academia are not drawn separately; they appear as industries, such as colleges and universities and scientific research and development services.

    What the position means

    East-west and north-south have no meaning of their own. Industries are close when they employ a similar, distinctive mix of occupations and when their descriptions are similar. Distances are approximate: a high-dimensional similarity is squeezed into two dimensions, and sectors are then set on an even, lightly jittered grid so the hills fill the land. A detailed industry can sit outside its sector's area when it resembles industries elsewhere.

    Sources

    • Industries, employment, growth: BLS National Employment Matrix, 2025 and projected 2035 (NAICS 2022).
    • Value added: BEA GDP by Industry, 2025. The sector figures are BEA's; below the sector level each BEA industry is split by payroll (BLS QCEW, 2025), so those figures are ≈ estimates. Owner-occupied housing is excluded. Federal value added is mostly government pay and has no uniformed military in the job counts, so its value per job reads high.
    • Similarity: occupation mixes from the Employment Matrix, occupation descriptions from O*NET 31.0, and text-embedding similarity.
    • Deep zones (Level 2): a few industries are split finer than any official table. Sizes come from QCEW 2025 employment, IPEDS 2023 staff and published headcounts, all ≈ estimates.

    Levels

    Level 0 shows 20 industry sectors. Zoom in, or use the Detail control, for 304 detailed industries (Level 1), and then the deep zones (Level 2). Finer nodes replace their parent.

    Limits

    This is a descriptive map of the present, not a forecast or advice. Classification boundaries, estimates and similarity scores all carry error; the sources and rules are in the project's repository (data/SOURCES.md, SPEC.md).
